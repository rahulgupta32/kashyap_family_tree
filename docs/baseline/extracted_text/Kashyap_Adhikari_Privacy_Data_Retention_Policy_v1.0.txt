Approval scope: Jyphra Technology Pvt. Ltd. has approved this document as the internal SDLC privacy and data-retention baseline. This owner approval is not external legal advice. Nepal-qualified legal validation of statutory interpretation, notices, consent language, children's data rules, cross-border processing and retention periods remains a mandatory production-release condition.

Document purpose
This package translates the project requirements into a privacy operating model, user-facing notice baseline, retention schedule, rights workflow, breach process and engineering acceptance criteria. It deliberately applies stricter controls where genealogy, family disputes, children, exact location, contact details or cultural relationships can create lasting harm.

1. Executive policy decision
The platform shall operate as a privacy-by-design, evidence-based genealogy and community service. Personal data is collected only for defined purposes, access is scoped by role and branch, sensitive relationships are protected, and every privileged action is auditable. Public visibility is never the default for a living person.
Release decision: Privacy, retention, account deletion, consent, children's protections, processor contracts, incident response and store disclosures are Release 1.0 launch requirements. They are not post-launch enhancements.

1.1 Non-negotiable rules
No genealogy fact is automatically public merely because it concerns family history.
No biological, adoptive, step, guardian, disputed or former-spouse relationship is inferred or collapsed into another category.
No precise location, private contact, identity document, private message or minor's profile is exposed through search, maps or exports by default.
No account deletion request is treated as permission to corrupt shared genealogy; the account and private data are deleted or anonymized while necessary shared historical claims are separated, minimized and retained only under an approved basis.
No production database, log, backup or analytics export may have an undefined owner or indefinite retention period.
No administrator may bypass consent, disclosure, retention or audit controls without a recorded, time-limited emergency authorization.
1.2 Approval conditions
Jyphra Technology Pvt. Ltd. is the policy owner and approving organization, and this version is effective as the internal SDLC baseline from 8 September 2026. Before production release, the company must record its official address, privacy contact, complaint channel, authorized legal representative and data-hosting/processor locations. A Nepal-qualified lawyer must validate the final legal basis, statutory mappings and public-facing notices.

6. Consent, notice and choice
Consent must be specific, informed, freely given where relied upon, recorded and withdrawable. Consent is not bundled with unrelated processing. A withdrawal stops future optional processing without invalidating necessary prior records or approved legal retention.
Show a concise notice at the point of collection, with a link to the full policy.
Separate choices for public profile visibility, exact/coarse location, photos/media, notifications, research/analytics and optional directory listing.
Record notice version, language, purpose, decision, timestamp, actor and withdrawal history.
Provide Nepali and English notices with equivalent meaning; resolve any legal-language precedence explicitly.
Do not use preselected optional consent or make optional visibility a condition of account creation.
When one member submits information about another person, treat it as a claim requiring notice, verification and appropriate visibility—not as the other person's consent.
6.1 Children and young persons
Until legal counsel approves a specific age and verification model, accounts identified as belonging to minors are restricted. The system must prioritize the child's best interests, obtain verified parent/guardian authorization where required, limit discoverability, disable exact location and public contact, restrict direct messaging from unknown adults, and provide rapid reporting/removal channels. Age must not be inferred solely from appearance or name.
7. Genealogy-specific privacy controls
Display only the minimum fields allowed by the viewer's relationship, branch, consent and authority scope.
Represent claims, sources, confidence and disputes separately; do not overwrite competing evidence.
Hide sensitive edge types from unauthorized viewers even when adjacent people are visible.
Apply anti-enumeration controls to search and APIs; rate-limit traversal and prevent bulk graph extraction.
For deceased persons, still protect surviving relatives, private correspondence, disputed claims and restricted sources.
When a person requests account deletion, separate account credentials and contributed content from independently sourced shared genealogy. Delete or anonymize what is not required; retain only approved minimum facts with a documented reason and challenge mechanism.
Nata/Saino, Jutho, Tithi/Shraddha and other cultural outputs must show the rule version and must not reveal hidden relationships through inference.
8. Transparency notice baseline
The production privacy notice must clearly identify the organization, contact details, categories of information, sources, purposes, recipients, hosting locations, retention, security, user choices, complaint method, children's protections, deletion/correction process and effective date. It must be available before sign-up and from app settings and the public website.
Confirmed: legal entity / policy owner - Jyphra Technology Pvt. Ltd.; internal SDLC effective date - 8 September 2026. Remaining publication placeholders: [REGISTRATION/ADDRESS], [PRIVACY CONTACT], [COMPLAINT CHANNEL], [HOSTING COUNTRIES], [PROCESSOR LIST] and [COUNSEL APPROVAL REFERENCE]. 9. Data-subject and member request workflow
Receive: provide in-app and web routes for access, correction, consent withdrawal, objection/restriction, portability where offered, and account/data deletion.
Authenticate: verify identity proportionately without collecting excessive new documentation.
Scope: identify account data, genealogy claims, messages, media, audit/security records, processors and backups affected.
Assess: determine what can be fulfilled, what must be retained, and whether another person's rights would be affected.
Approve: DPO or delegated privacy authority records the decision and legal/policy basis.
Execute: delete, anonymize, correct, restrict or export across production systems and instruct processors.
Communicate: confirm outcome, retained categories/reasons, expected backup expiry and appeal/complaint route.
Audit: retain a minimal request record without retaining the deleted content itself.
Target service standard: acknowledge within 3 business days and complete ordinary verified requests within 30 calendar days. This is an internal service target, not a statement of Nepal's statutory deadline; legal counsel must confirm applicable deadlines and exceptions.

The process must delete or anonymize associated data that is not legally or operationally required, request deletion from processors, and clearly explain any narrowly retained categories and periods.

12. Legal holds and archival exceptions
A legal hold suspends scheduled deletion only for specifically identified records and must record issuer, authority, scope, reason, date, review date and release. Holds must not become indefinite convenience archives. Cultural or historical value alone does not justify retaining a living person's private contact, exact address, messages or sensitive relationship status.
13. Security requirements
Encrypt sensitive data in transit and at rest; manage keys separately with rotation and access logging.
Use MFA for privileged accounts, strong password hashing, short-lived sessions, revocation and device/session visibility.
Enforce authorization server-side at record and field level; branch scope is never trusted from the client.
Separate production, staging and development; do not use real personal data in development or demos.
Use immutable centralized audit logs that administrators cannot silently alter.
Scan uploads; validate file types; remove unsafe metadata; use signed, expiring download links.
Maintain secure backups, tested restoration, disaster recovery, vulnerability management, penetration testing and dependency review.
Prevent sensitive values in application logs, analytics, crash reports, URLs, notifications and support screenshots.
Apply rate limits, anti-scraping, export approval, anomaly detection and download watermarking where appropriate.
Require documented secure deletion and access termination when staff or processors leave.
14. Messaging, notifications and community safety
Private messages must be access-controlled and excluded from advertising or unrelated profiling. Whether end-to-end encryption is provided must be stated accurately; the system must not claim E2EE unless only endpoints hold the content keys and operational recovery/moderation implications are documented. Push notifications must avoid sensitive relationship, dispute or message content on lock screens by default.
Moderation access to private content is case-based, logged and limited to reported content or a documented safety/security basis. Reports, appeals and sanctions use the separation-of-duties framework.
15. Location and map privacy
Use manually selected locality or coarse coordinates by default; do not derive exact residence from background GPS.
Require a separate, reversible opt-in before showing a household or member on a map.
Apply geographic jitter, aggregation or minimum-group thresholds for member-facing maps.
Do not show minor, vulnerable, disputed or protected-person locations.
Prevent export of map coordinates except through a separately approved administrative purpose.
When location permission is denied, provide a manual non-location alternative.
16. Vendors, hosting and cross-border processing
Before any processor receives personal data, the organization must document the service, data categories, locations, subprocessors, security, breach notification, deletion/return, audit rights, availability and exit plan. Cross-border hosting or support must be reviewed by Nepal-qualified counsel and disclosed appropriately. Production must not depend on an unreviewed personal developer account.

17. Incident and breach response
Detect and open an incident record; preserve evidence without spreading sensitive data.
Contain access, tokens, accounts, integrations and compromised devices.
Assess affected people, records, sensitivity, geography, duration and likely harm.
Notify the Incident Commander, Security Authority and DPO immediately; involve legal counsel for notification duties.
Communicate to affected people and authorities when legally required or risk-appropriate, using accurate approved content.
Recover from a trusted state, monitor recurrence and verify data integrity.
Complete a blameless post-incident review with actions, owners, deadlines and effectiveness testing.
Internal target: Severity 1 privacy/security incidents are acknowledged immediately, command is established within 15 minutes, and executive/DPO escalation occurs within 30 minutes. Legal notification deadlines must be confirmed by counsel.
